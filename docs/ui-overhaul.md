# The reward art system

The fan app's visual language is a **game skin**, not a dashboard theme: chunky
display titles, struck-metal medallions, sticker tilts, tactile keys. This
document covers the part of it that is drawn — the rewards — and where the line
falls between what the platform owns and what a club does.

For colors and the re-skin mechanism, read [`branding.md`](./branding.md) first.
For motion, [`animations.md`](./animations.md). This is the layer between them.

## One component, two axes

Every rosette, medal, ribbon, cup, voucher, keychain, map pin and lens badge in
the app is [`RewardMedallion.vue`](../app/src/components/reward/RewardMedallion.vue)
with different props:

| Axis | Values | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| `shape` | rosette · medal · ribbon · cup · ticket · keychain · pin · lens | what the object **is** |
| `tier` | locked · bronze · silver · gold · rare | what it is **made of** |

Eight shapes across five tiers is forty pictures. As forty files it would be
forty things to redraw when the brand moves, and a standing invitation for the
bronze cup and the bronze rosette to drift into different alloys. As two props
it is one gradient, defined once, that every shape reads.

Add a shape by adding a `<g>` and a `FACES` entry. Add a tier by adding three
custom properties and one union member — the drawing does not change.

See the whole matrix at **`/dev/rewards`** in a dev build. That route lives
inside an `import.meta.env.DEV` branch in the router, so Rollup drops the route
and the chunk from production.

## The blend: platform metal, club crest

> The platform owns the metal. The club owns the crest.

- **Metals are platform-semantic and never tenant-themed.** `--color-tier-*`
  sits alongside mint in `main.css` and is not generated by `lib/color.ts`.
  Silver has to read as silver at every venue, or a rarity ladder means one
  thing at the ballpark and another at the vineyard, and nothing across the
  platform.
- **The crest inside the frame is the tenant's accent gradient.** That is the
  half that re-skins. Switch brands at `/admin/branding` and every medallion in
  the app changes its face while the metals hold still.
- **`crest` overrides the face with a flat color**, used only where the caller's
  item already has an identity color — a mission's own color on the venue map,
  where eight identical accent discs would be useless. Not a general escape
  hatch.
- **A locked reward takes metal even when a crest was passed.** An explicit
  color must never leak the identity of a reward the fan has not earned.

`rare` is the one tier that is not a metal: its mid stop jumps to cyan, because
a hue shift across the sweep is how foil behaves and how every trading card in
the target age bracket signals rarity.

## The fan ground paints before the app does

`#app-backdrop` — the tinted surface the fan app sits on — lives in
`index.html` and is styled from `main.css`. That placement is the whole point.

As a Vue component it could not exist until the router's async guard had
resolved a session and fetched a brand, several hundred milliseconds after
first paint, so every cold load showed a flat near-white page and then snapped
to a gradient. In the document it is painted in the FIRST frame, from a
render-blocking stylesheet, with the tenant's colors already applied — the
store writes them onto `<html>` synchronously during setup. Easing the old one
in would only have made a late arrival graceful.

**`#app-backdrop` is `position: fixed; z-index: 0`, and that has a sharp
edge.** CSS paints positioned elements above the backgrounds of non-positioned
blocks, so a full-page layout that means to cover the fan ground must be
*positioned above it*, not merely opaque. `BareLayout` and `ConsoleShell` carry
`relative z-10` for exactly this reason; without it the backdrop paints over
their entire page and the content vanishes. `AppShell` was never affected — its
`main` is already `relative z-10` and its bars are `z-20`.

## The showcase ground

`.showcase` is the app's one dark surface — the trophy shelf and the win
celebration. It is **not a dark mode**: it never follows the system setting and
never spreads past the component that opts in. The app is used outdoors in
daylight, and the contrast grading in `branding.md` is done against the light
canvas.

The ground is platform-fixed; the spotlight in it is the tenant's accent, so a
club's own color lights its own silverware. Display titles on it need
`.display-title--on-dark`, which swaps the sticker outline to the surface color
— the base recipe strokes in `brand-900`, which vanishes on a dark ground.

## The Huntima mark

[`HuntimaMark.vue`](../app/src/components/HuntimaMark.vue) is real path data,
inlined. It replaced an `app/src/assets/logo.svg` that was a **595 KB base64
PNG wearing an `.svg` extension**, fetched on every screen (header and nav tab)
and resampled down to 28 px. The file is still on disk and no longer imported;
if a genuine vector export exists, it is the better source and should replace
the component's paths.

Its colors are **fixed**. It is the one mark in the app that does not re-skin,
for the same reason the display face does not: it is the platform's identity,
not a club's. Do not wire those hexes to brand tokens.

## Levels and the venue map

Two additive, optional fields on `missionSchema`, both display-only:

- **`group`** — a level/chapter name. Missions sharing one are grouped under a
  collapsible header with its own meter. It never *gates* a mission: a fan can
  win level 3 before level 1, because a locked ladder in a stadium means
  everyone queues behind whichever mission's subject wandered off.
- **`geo`** — where the mission is in the real world: `{ lat, lng,
  radiusMeters }`. `radiusMeters: 0` shows an exact pin; a positive value shows
  a "somewhere in here" circle, so a city-wide hunt can hint an area instead of
  a doorstep.

Staff set it in the hunt editor (`MissionGeoField.vue`) by clicking a map or
typing coordinates. Fans see every located mission on the hunt map
(`MissionMap.vue`), and each pin/area routes to that mission.

### The map does not open the geofencing seam

The map is **wayfinding**, drawn with Leaflet over OpenStreetMap's free,
keyless tiles. That buys:

- no paid tile plan — OSM public tiles need no API key (a dedicated tile host
  is a one-line swap for heavy production traffic)
- real streets for a city-wide hunt, and a legible area circle when a mission
  wants to hint rather than pinpoint
- **no location permission to draw a map**

There is no "you are here" dot. Whether a fan is really at the venue stays
exactly where it was: the soft `useGeofence` check against `tenantConfig.venue`
at capture time. A mission's `geo` is wayfinding; verification is a separate
decision.

## What was deliberately not built

The mockups these components came from also show a daily bonus chest that grants
points, a scout-points balance, a user level, rarity percentages, and a
leaderboard. **None of it is built.**

Every one of them needs a server that owns the badge ledger and issues rewards,
and this one does not yet — see
[`architecture.md` § Seams left open](./architecture.md#seams-left-open). A
rewards grid nothing can actually award is a screen that lies to a child, and a
leaderboard is not a UI feature with a backend attached: it is a decision about
storing ranked behavioural data on minors.

What shipped instead is the same picture fed by things that actually happened:

| Mockup asks for | What it became | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| Reward case with locked `?` slots | one slot per mission in the hunt | the published mission list |
| Daily bonus chest | the hunt-complete celebration | a server-verified capture |
| Badge series grids | one row per hunt played | badges per campaign, on device |
| Journey timeline | dated joins and wins | timestamps already stored |
| Leaderboard, points, level, rarity | *not built* | — |

The chest is the clearest case. It exists, it opens, it is the payoff object
from the mockup — it just stands on the moment the server confirmed the capture
that finished the hunt, rather than on a timer.

## The celebration owns the screen

`CaptureCelebration.vue` teleports to `<body>` and covers everything: the
showcase ground, a slow ray sweep, confetti measured in viewport units, the
headline in the display face, and two ways out. Two variants — a gold rosette
in the mission's own color for a badge, the chest for the hunt that just
ended, because escalating every capture to treasure is how the last one stops
meaning anything.

It owns its own theatre (confetti, chest timing, rays) rather than taking them
as props: `CapturePage` decides *whether* a celebration happens, this decides
what one looks like. Body scroll is locked while it is up, focus moves into
it, and Escape takes the quiet exit rather than the prize counter — a
dismissal should never be the thing that navigates somebody to a reward.
